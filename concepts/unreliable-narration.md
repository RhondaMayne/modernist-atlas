Unreliable Narration
Definition

Unreliable narration occurs when a narrator's account cannot be accepted as completely trustworthy. The narrator may misunderstand events, omit information, contradict themselves, or present a version of events shaped by limited knowledge, bias, or self-deception. Readers must therefore interpret the narrative critically rather than accepting it at face value.

Why it Matters

Unreliable narration is a defining feature of many modernist novels because it shifts authority away from an omniscient narrator towards subjective consciousness. Rather than presenting a single objective reality, modernist texts often require readers to reconstruct meaning from competing perspectives, fragmented memories, and uncertain truths.
