# Leonard Orr, "Modernism and the Issue of Periodization" (2005)

## 1. Citation

Orr, Leonard. "Modernism and the Issue of Periodization." *CLCWeb: Comparative Literature and Culture* 7, no. 1 (2005): Article 3. https://doi.org/10.7771/1481-4374.1254.

**Date read:** 17 November 2026

## 2. Summary

Leonard Orr examines the conceptual and practical difficulties of dividing literary history into distinct periods. He surveys competing approaches to periodisation, including historical chronology, literary movements, and formal characteristics, demonstrating that scholars frequently challenge established period categories while continuing to employ them.

Focusing on modernism, Orr considers the instability of chronological boundaries, the relationship between modernism and postmodernism, and the exclusionary consequences of established definitions. He concludes that neither increasingly refined categories nor the abandonment of chronological distinctions adequately resolves the problem.

## 3. Central Argument

Literary periodisation remains conceptually unstable yet institutionally entrenched. Although scholars recognise that literary movements resist fixed historical boundaries and coherent definitions, periods continue to organise teaching, publishing, criticism, and literary history.

Orr exposes the tension between the intellectual limitations of period categories and their persistent practical usefulness. He considers treating them as provisional, heuristic frameworks but does not offer a definitive solution.

## 4. Key Concepts

**Periodisation:** The organisation of literary history into periods defined through chronological, historical, cultural, or aesthetic characteristics.

**Historical versus formal definitions:** The distinction between classifying literature according to when it was produced and classifying it according to its stylistic techniques.

**Canonical exclusion:** The tendency for established definitions of literary movements to privilege certain writers, traditions, and practices while marginalising others.

**Circular definition:** The process by which selected canonical writers establish the characteristics of a movement, which are subsequently used to determine which other writers belong to it.

**Heuristic categories:** Provisional classifications used to facilitate interpretation, rather than definitive descriptions of literary history.

## 5. Strongest Evidence

**The persistence of conventional categories (pp. 2–3):** Orr demonstrates that journals, anthologies, and academic institutions retain period divisions even while publishing scholarship that questions their intellectual validity.

**Susan Stanford Friedman's critique (p. 3):** Definitions of modernism can become circular when the characteristics of selected canonical writers determine the boundaries of the movement.

**Thomas Hardy and James Joyce (pp. 4–5):** Orr demonstrates the instability of period distinctions through writers whose works display characteristics associated with different literary movements.

**The modernism/postmodernism debate (pp. 5–6):** Competing classifications of individual works, including *Ulysses*, reveal the difficulty of assigning stable chronological or stylistic identities to literary texts.

## 6. Connections

### Primary Texts

**Virginia Woolf, *Jacob's Room*.** Woolf's formal experimentation illustrates how literary techniques can become concentrated and developed within particular historical circumstances without necessarily originating within those circumstances.

**James Joyce, *Dubliners* and *Ulysses*.** Orr's discussion of Joyce demonstrates the difficulty of assigning a writer, or even an individual work, to a single literary period.

**Anna Kavan, later experimental fiction.** Kavan's work invites consideration of modernist practices that persist beyond the conventional historical boundaries of modernism.

### Concepts

**Modernism and modernity:** The distinction between formal literary experimentation and the historical and cultural conditions within which that experimentation develops.

**Literary periodisation:** The usefulness and limitations of organising literary history through chronological and aesthetic categories.

**Canon formation:** The role of established writers and texts in determining the characteristics subsequently associated with modernism.

### Thinkers

**Susan Stanford Friedman:** Her analysis of binary, circular, and metonymic definitions exposes how literary categories can reproduce established hierarchies.

**Fredric Jameson:** His work demonstrates the tension between critiques of historical periodisation and its continuing interpretative necessity.

**Marjorie Perloff:** Her account of modernism's continuing aesthetic practices challenges the conventional succession from modernism to postmodernism.

## 8. Critique

### Strengths

Orr provides a valuable overview of competing approaches to literary periodisation and demonstrates their conceptual inconsistencies.

His discussion of canonical exclusion is particularly important, since definitions of modernism may reproduce established literary hierarchies.

He also demonstrates that literary practices do not necessarily conform to the chronological boundaries imposed by literary history.

His examination of the institutional persistence of period categories reveals the tension between scholarly criticism and academic organisation.

### Limitations

Although Orr identifies significant conceptual difficulties, his discussion offers limited practical guidance concerning how literary scholars might employ period categories productively.

Periodisation remains essential for situating literature within its historical, cultural, and social circumstances. Its usefulness does not depend upon establishing precise boundaries or demonstrating that all writers within a period share identical aesthetic practices.

Modernism may be understood as a period characterised by the particular intensity, accumulation, and interaction of experimental literary techniques within specific historical conditions. These techniques did not necessarily originate during the modernist period, nor did they disappear when it ended.

The limitations of periodisation may therefore be inherent in literary historical interpretation rather than problems requiring definitive resolution. Categories should be evaluated according to their interpretative usefulness, rather than their capacity to impose comprehensive or internally consistent classifications.

Nevertheless, recognising the provisional nature of period categories must not become an excuse for preserving definitions that systematically exclude particular writers or traditions.

**Candidate permanent note: Periodisation as a Heuristic Framework**

Literary periodisation is most productive when understood as a provisional method of historical and cultural interpretation rather than a rigid system of classification. Modernism may be distinguished by a concentration of experimental practices within particular historical circumstances, without assuming that those practices originated or ended within its conventional chronological boundaries.

Period categories should remain open to revision as scholarship examines previously marginalised writers, traditions, and artistic practices.
